# Full rulebook from the Oct 27 2026 image masterclass

Source: John Aspinall, L10 Sales Team meeting, Sound Around / Pyle USA. Everything below is what he said, organized so Claude can apply it.

## Why images first

- Amazon is split-testing a page with no A+ and no brand story: hero plus stack only. If the hero and stack are weak, nothing else gets seen.
- CTR vs conversion: both matter, but CTR is the cheaper lever. CTR is driven by title, price, reviews, coupon, and the hero image. Only the hero is freely changeable. Conversion depends on dozens of things so you cannot tell what moved it.
- Doubling CTR with a steady conversion rate doubles revenue off one image change.
- The one time high CTR plus low conversion happens: the hero oversells or lies (900 lb in the hero, 700 lb in the stack). Keep claims consistent.
- Time split: 50% hero, 35% stack, 15% everything else.

## Hero image

1. Always open the search results page for the main keyword first. See what the top sellers show and what none of them show.
2. Know your SERP shape. 1-by (appliances): one item per row, hard to stand out. 5-by (toys, outdoor): wide grid, more organic real estate, but everyone looks identical.
3. The hero must carry the ONE value prop that decommoditizes the product. Examples from the session:
   - Bucket towel warmer: two colors exist. Show a color swatch so the buyer who dislikes white knows black is there.
   - Dual basket air fryer: "4.4 qt + 4.4 qt = 8.8 qt, 9 presets". Make the numbers big.
   - Saucer swing: 900 lb capacity, three colors. Put a post-edited label on the front lip plus swatches.
   - Pizza maker: temperature control 260F to 430F (the thing the single-temp sibling cannot do). Color options if any.
   - Dog ball launcher: launches up to 39 ft, 3 balls included, rechargeable, all day park play.
4. Text callouts must be readable at phone thumbnail size. A label you cannot read is wasted pixels and may be a suppression risk for nothing.
5. Brown box packaging: put the brown box behind the product and print the value prop and swatches on it. Do not dress it up as retail packaging. This is the safest place to add text and angles (dials, knobs, top view) without a suppression.
6. Post-edits (labels added in editing that are not on the physical product) are fine for clicks as long as they are large, on-brand, and true.
7. Thumbnail vs full image aspect ratio must match. A slim thumbnail that opens wide creates doubt about what the product really looks like.
8. Parentage: children must be the same product with a real variation (color, size). A digital-display model is not a variation of an analog one. Two listings of the same item with different internal names and prices confuse the buyer and will be split by Amazon's bot. The hero is anchored to the parent's primary child, so a weird child drags the whole family.
9. Realistic, not fantasy. A woman getting blasted in the face by leaves and scent is not a towel warmer image.
10. Rechargeable products: show USB-C. Micro-USB reads as old and buyers skip it.

## Image stack (slots 2 through 7 or 8)

Core test for every image: what does this image say? Then: can the 70-year-old on a phone in sunlight read it?

Checklist per image:
- One message per image, stated in large text. Count the messages across the stack; duplicates are wasted slots.
- Product and text fill the frame. Small sub-diagrams and three-step strips are the most common failure. Use the full image height for each step if needed.
- Realistic use. Normal amounts of food. Nothing the product cannot do (whole chicken in a small basket, seafood platter that did not come from that fryer, quiche in a pizza maker unless it truly works).
- Dimensions: always show why the size matters (fits under the cabinet, on the microwave shelf, in the trunk). Do not list numbers on a blank background.
- Cleanup: dishwasher-safe parts shown on the top rack. Hand-wash parts shown in the sink. Not propped on a counter.
- Commoditized claims (non-stick, durable, easy setup) need an us-vs-them or before/after. "Cheap one: fish-stick flavored crust. Ours: wipes clean."
- Translate every spec into a buyer benefit in US language. Inches not millimeters. "Soft, safe, tennis-ball size" not "58 mm ETPU". "Charger you already own" not "USB-C cable included". "3 to 4 hours of play" not "rechargeable battery".
- Accessories are not value props. Nobody buys a swing for the storage bag. Lead with the use case (kid outside off the tablet, reading under a tree) not the bag.
- Education sells: distance from the tree trunk, which branch thickness, what temperature for which dish. Give the buyer the answer they would otherwise have to Google.
- Spec ranges need concrete examples across the range. For a 260F to 430F pizza maker: 260 eggs and pancakes, 325 quiche and cinnamon rolls, 375 fruit pie and quesadilla, 430 pizza. One four-panel image, degrees under each panel.
- Order matters. Lead with the strongest differentiator, not the last slot. Alternate the mood (fried, healthy, fried) so the stack does not feel like one long ad.
- Pull strong real photos out of A+ into the stack. A+ is "nice to know". The stack is "need to know".
- AI images must not look AI. No cartoon pizza, no plastic kids, no robot-posed dogs, no weird dog breeds. Use real-looking common dogs: labs, goldens, dalmatians. Never pit bulls or German shepherds.
- Lifestyle-only heroes from China-direct sellers get suppressed. Do not copy them.
- Amazon reads text on images (OCR) and Alexa shows them as proof when asked "is this good for X". Treat image text like SEO copy: real claims, real words.
- Do not spin "cook, cook, cook" across every image. Vary the headline verb and the backgrounds so the stack reads as a story, not a loop.
- Weight capacity claims: show it. Two adults plus two kids beats three small kids with a "900 lb" label. Or one kid plus a visual that adds up to the number.

## A+ content

Still build it (Premium A+ is uploaded via the seller account and flows to the vendor listing at no premium fee). But any image that is strong enough to sell should be duplicated into the stack. Never let the best photo live only in A+.

## Brand kit (needed before scaling)

One per brand: Pyle, SereneLife, NutriChef, Hurtle. Contains:
- Primary and secondary colors (hex)
- Headline and body fonts
- Logo lockup and where it sits
- Label style for post-edits (shape, fill, text size minimums for mobile)
- Image mix rule for a stack, e.g. slot 2 value prop, slot 3 us vs them, slot 4 dimensions in place, slot 5 lifestyle, slot 6 how-to full height, slot 7 cleanup, slot 8 what's in the box only if it earns the slot
- Photography style (background color, lighting, model demographics, pet breeds allowed)

Without a kit the hero, stack and A+ drift apart and look like three different brands. With a kit, AI can regenerate a stack in about 15 minutes and roll it out across siblings in a few hours.

## Rollout logic

Fix one listing perfectly. Then write the rule for siblings:
- Locked: layout, fonts, colors, headline structure, image order, background, model types.
- Adaptive: the numbers (baskets, quarts, feet, pounds), the color swatches, the product render.
- A 3-basket sibling gets "3 baskets, one meal" and three capacity callouts with a new total, not a copy of the 2-basket image.

## Split testing

- Pre-Amazon polls (PickFu, Product Opinion) on a sample of the product line to learn what wins before spending traffic.
- On Amazon, 4 weeks is usually too short.
- Always test against a version that fixes the biggest finding, not a sibling of the current bad image. Otherwise you crown the "winning bad image".

## Learning the product

Buyers manage many categories and cannot be experts on all of them. Before auditing, build a quick persona: who buys this, what pain, what are they comparing against, what would make them nervous (will the dog swallow the ball, will the kids get hurt, will it fit). Pull questions from reviews and the SERP. Then audit.
